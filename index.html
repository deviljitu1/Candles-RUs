THE FOUNDATIONS01 / 03

Start with the candle
you want to make.

Before you choose a wax, think about the candle itself. Will it sit inside a container, or stand on its own? That question helps you narrow down what to look for.

A CANDLE IN A JAR

Look for a container wax.

Check that the wax is intended for container candles and read the supplier’s guidance.

A FREESTANDING CANDLE

Look for a pillar wax.

A freestanding candle needs a wax designed to hold its shape outside a vessel.

✳

A useful first note: “What am I making, and is this wax designed for it?”

THE WORKING NOTES02 / 03

Your next batch starts
with a better note.

A record is useful when future-you can understand it. Give each test its own ID, and keep the materials, process, and observations together.

Batch ID
TEST–003 · Illustrative entry
Materials
Record exact wax, wick, fragrance, and vessel IDs.
Process
Record weights, temperatures, and dates as measured.
Observation
“Scent seemed lighter than the previous test.”
Next question
“Did I use the same materials and conditions?”
✳

Write what happened first. Keep your explanation as a question until you’ve tested it.

THE NEXT EXPERIMENT03 / 03

A result is a clue.
Give it some context.

“This one was better” is a start. A more useful note says what you noticed, which batch you compared it with, and what changed.

  1. 1

    Describe the observation.

    What did you see or notice? When did you notice it?

  2. 2

    Check the batch records.

    Compare materials, measurements, process, and conditions.

  3. 3

    Choose one question for next time.

    Keep the comparison focused and follow your suppliers’ material and testing instructions.

✳

These notes demonstrate a learning approach. They do not replace a full burn-testing procedure.
